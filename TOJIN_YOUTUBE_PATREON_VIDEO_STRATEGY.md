# Tojin Games — YouTube + Patreon Video Strategy

_Last updated: 2026-09-17_

This file defines the default content strategy for all Tojin Games repositories.  
When creating trailers, Shorts, devlogs, gameplay videos, thumbnails, titles, descriptions, or scripts, follow these principles unless a specific project explicitly overrides them.

---

## 1. Channel Positioning

**Tojin Games = independent science, space and simulation games.**

Core identity:

> I build science, space and simulation games.

The projects should feel like parts of one studio rather than unrelated experiments.

Current examples:

- **Gravity 3D** — gravity, orbital dynamics, astrophysics, visual physics experiments
- **Orbit** — orbital mechanics, rendezvous, spacecraft navigation, realistic spaceflight
- **Jednota** — AI-driven 4X strategy, autonomous races, tactical fleet combat
- **Starforge / simulation projects** — systemic simulation, emergent behaviour, building and management

The YouTube channel is primarily a **discovery and acquisition channel**.

Primary funnel:

**YouTube Short → longer video / game showcase → playable game or demo → Patreon**

Patreon:

**Tojin Games | Independent science, space and simulation games**

Patreon should be treated as the main long-term supporter platform, not YouTube Ads.

---

## 2. Current Channel Lessons

Current channel state on 2026-09-17:

- ~150 subscribers
- ~13.5k lifetime views
- Shorts are the main discovery format
- Long-form generates disproportionately more watch time
- Paid promotion has generated a large part of total views, therefore raw view count alone is not a reliable success metric

Important distinction:

### Do not judge videos primarily by total views

Always distinguish:

- organic Shorts-feed views
- Browse / Suggested
- Search
- Subscribers / channel page
- external traffic
- paid Advertising traffic

A video with fewer total views but strong organic Shorts distribution is more valuable for future content strategy than a promoted video with several thousand paid views.

## 3. Best Performing Content Pattern

The strongest current pattern is:

> **Small input → surprising systemic consequence**

Examples:

- One small push changes an orbit
- Flying toward a spacecraft makes you fall behind
- A tiny gravity assist changes the trajectory dramatically
- Two black holes narrowly miss each other
- A spacecraft steals momentum from a planet
- A single tactical decision changes an entire fleet battle
- An AI civilization makes an unexpected strategic choice

This pattern should be used heavily.

The viewer should immediately understand:

1. **What is the question?**
2. **Why is the result surprising?**
3. **What happens?**
4. **Can I see or play this myself?**

---

## 4. Shorts Strategy

Target:

- **20–35 seconds**
- usually one idea only
- fast visual payoff
- very little setup
- no feature lists
- no long intro
- no studio logo intro
- no slow cinematic opening unless the cinematic itself is the hook

Preferred structure:

### 0–2 s — Hook

Start with the surprising claim, visual, or question.

Examples:

> Want to catch that spacecraft? Don't fly toward it.

> One tiny push can change an entire orbit.

> My AI admiral retreated without asking me.

> What happens if two black holes miss each other?

> Can you steal energy from Jupiter?

### 2–8 s — Setup

Show only the minimum context necessary.

### 8–25 s — Consequence

Let the simulation produce the result.

Prefer visible cause-and-effect over narration.

### Final 3–5 s — Payoff

Finish on the strongest image, result, destruction, collision, reveal, orbit, fleet outcome, or scientific insight.

Optional CTA:

> Play it yourself.

> Full simulation on the channel.

> Built in Gravity 3D.

Do not overload the final seconds with multiple CTAs.

## 5. Shorts Topics

### Gravity 3D

Strong concepts:

- What happens if an asteroid barely misses Jupiter?
- Can a tiny asteroid move a planet?
- Can you steal orbital energy?
- Why does a gravity assist accelerate you?
- What happens near a neutron star?
- What happens if two black holes miss?
- Can one small push destabilize an orbit?
- How close can you fly before gravity wins?
- What happens when orbital velocity is slightly wrong?
- Can a moon escape its planet?
- Can a planet capture a passing object?

Best angle:

**physics question first, game second**

Do not title Shorts primarily as:

> Gravity 3D gameplay #14

Prefer:

> One Tiny Push Can Change an Entire Orbit

---

### Orbit

Strong concepts:

- Flying toward the target makes you fall behind
- Why spacecraft do not fly directly toward each other
- How orbital rendezvous actually works
- Why accelerating can make you arrive later
- Why higher orbit means slower orbital motion
- How to catch a spacecraft without pointing at it
- Why space combat would be unintuitive
- How burns change an orbit instead of directly changing position

Best angle:

**counter-intuitive orbital mechanics**

Orbit has strong YouTube potential because many mechanics feel wrong until visually demonstrated.

---

### Jednota

Do not primarily market Jednota through feature lists.

Weak framing:

> 4X strategy with AI agents, tactical battles and procedural technology

Stronger framing:

> I gave every alien civilization its own AI.

> My AI admiral decided to retreat without asking me.

> Eight alien species. Eight different minds.

> The enemy convinced my ally to betray me.

> This AI civilization remembered what I did 200 turns ago.

> Every race negotiates differently.

> I tried to bluff an AI empire.

Focus on:

- emergent AI behaviour
- diplomacy
- betrayal
- tactical decisions
- unexpected retreats
- fleet sacrifices
- grudges and memory
- different species personalities
- asymmetric fleet doctrines

The viewer should first see a **story**.

The game mechanics explain why that story happened.

## 6. Long-Form Strategy

Current 1–2 minute videos are often too short to generate substantial watch time.

Preferred long-form duration:

**5–8 minutes**

Occasionally 8–15 minutes when the topic genuinely supports it.

Long videos should answer one clear question or tell one development story.

Example:

# I Built a Gravity Game Without a Physics Engine

Suggested structure:

### 0:00 — strongest result

Black-hole collision, extreme flyby, broken orbit, or another visual payoff.

### 0:10 — premise

> Everything you just saw comes from the same gravity simulation.

### 0:30 — basic mechanic

Show one simple object.

### 1:30 — larger system

Moon / planet.

### 2:30 — gravity assist

Show why it works.

### 3:30 — stars / neutron stars

Increase scale.

### 5:00 — black holes

Deliver the strongest spectacle.

### 6:00+ — game / player interaction

Show how the viewer can try it.

## 7. Recommended Content Mix

Default publishing mix:

- **3–5 Shorts per week**
- **1 long-form video every 1–2 weeks**
- occasional trailer when a major release deserves it
- Patreon posts for deeper development context, builds, screenshots, design notes and WIP

A project does not need a full trailer for every change.

One development milestone can generate:

- 3–10 Shorts
- 1 development video
- screenshots / GIFs
- Patreon post
- later a polished trailer

Reuse production effort aggressively.

---

## 8. YouTube → Patreon Funnel

YouTube should create curiosity.

Patreon should convert the most interested viewers into supporters.

Recommended funnel:

**Short**
→ surprising physics / AI / simulation moment

**Long-form**
→ deeper explanation / development / story

**Game**
→ viewer experiences the system directly

**Patreon**
→ supports continued development and gets relevant supporter benefits

Do not make every video feel like an advertisement for Patreon.

The content must work without the CTA.

Patreon CTA should be secondary:

> If you want to support continued development, Tojin Games is on Patreon.

When relevant:

> Builds, development posts and project updates are available through Patreon.

## 9. Titles

### Prefer

- curiosity
- contradiction
- strong consequence
- one clear idea
- understandable language
- minimal jargon

Examples:

> Flying Toward It Makes You Fall Behind

> One Tiny Push Can Break an Orbit

> I Gave Every Alien Race Its Own AI

> My AI Admiral Refused to Fight

> Can You Steal Energy From Jupiter?

> Two Black Holes Missed. This Happened.

### Avoid

- long feature lists
- version numbers
- generic project updates
- excessive hashtags in title
- technical terminology unless it is itself the hook
- naming the game before explaining why the viewer should care

Weak:

> JEDNOTA — Fleet Battles: Cinematic & Tactical 4X Strategy Gameplay

Better:

> My AI Admiral Knew the Battle Was Lost

Then place **Jednota** in the thumbnail, description, or subtitle.

---

## 10. Thumbnails

Thumbnail should communicate one event.

Prefer:

- one large object
- one strong interaction
- obvious direction of motion
- large scale difference
- ship vs planet
- asteroid vs black hole
- two fleets facing each other
- one AI character/species as a focal point

Avoid:

- UI-heavy screenshots
- tiny text
- many ships with no clear focal point
- full game interfaces
- multiple unrelated scenes
- feature lists

Thumbnail text, if any:

**1–4 words**

Examples:

- WRONG WAY
- TOO CLOSE
- RUN
- GRAVITY WINS
- AI RETREATED
- ONE PUSH
- NO ESCAPE

---

## 11. Video Descriptions

First two lines matter most.

Start with the concept, not metadata.

Example:

> A spacecraft ahead of you looks easy to catch. Burn directly toward it and you may actually fall farther behind.

Then explain the project.

Recommended order:

1. hook / explanation
2. game context
3. play link
4. Patreon
5. technical detail
6. music / attribution
7. hashtags

Do not put a large technical feature list before the viewer understands the video.

---

## 12. Science Content Rule

Tojin Games has a potential niche that combines:

**game development + real science + visual simulation**

Use this deliberately.

When a real physical phenomenon can be demonstrated visually:

- show the result first
- explain it simply
- keep the underlying simulation realistic
- avoid fake cinematic behaviour when a real physical result is available
- clearly distinguish real physics from fictional extrapolation

Technical depth can exist in:

- description
- long-form explanation
- Patreon post
- repository documentation

Shorts should usually remain visually understandable without equations.

## 13. Development Philosophy for Video-Friendly Games

When implementing new systems, consider whether they can create shareable visual moments.

Good systems produce:

- unexpected but explainable outcomes
- large scale differences
- chain reactions
- emergent AI behaviour
- catastrophic near misses
- recoveries from failure
- visually obvious cause-and-effect
- before/after states

When possible, provide development tools for capturing these moments:

- deterministic seeds
- replay
- time controls
- cinematic camera
- clean HUD mode
- simulation pause
- slow motion
- fast forward
- event markers
- reproducible scenarios

A feature that produces strong emergent scenes can be both good gameplay and good marketing.

## 14. ComfyUI / RTX 5090 Usage

Use the RTX 5090 + ComfyUI pipeline primarily to improve presentation, not to hide gameplay.

Best uses:

- cinematic intro shots
- race portraits
- atmospheric establishing shots
- transitions
- planet / space backgrounds
- promotional stills
- Patreon artwork
- thumbnail assets
- concept art
- short narrative inserts

Gameplay and simulation should remain recognizable as the actual game.

For science-oriented projects, avoid making generated cinematic footage look like simulated gameplay if it is not.

For Jednota, cinematic AI-generated shots can complement tactical footage very effectively.

## 15. Metrics That Matter

When reviewing a video, prioritize:

### Shorts

1. organic Shorts-feed views
2. average percentage viewed
3. rewatches
4. likes / engagement
5. subscribers gained
6. whether YouTube continues distributing it after the initial test

### Long-form

1. click-through rate
2. first 30-second retention
3. average view duration
4. total watch time
5. Suggested / Browse traffic
6. subscribers gained
7. comments / meaningful engagement

### Do not optimize around

- paid Advertising views
- raw total views without traffic-source context
- vanity subscriber growth from promotion alone

Paid campaigns may be useful for game awareness, but they should be analysed separately from organic YouTube performance.

## 17. Default Creative Test

Before publishing a video, answer:

### Hook

Can the first 2 seconds make sense to someone who has never heard of Tojin Games?

### Story

Is there one central question or event?

### Visuals

Can I understand the result without reading a paragraph?

### Length

Can this be shorter without losing the payoff?

### Title

Does the title describe why the viewer should care instead of merely naming the project?

### Funnel

If someone loves this video, is there an obvious next thing to watch, play, or support?

If any answer is **no**, revise before publishing.

---

## 18. Core Rule

The games are not the content.

**The interesting things that happen inside the games are the content.**

Do not start with:

> Here is my new game.

Start with:

> Look what happened.

Then reveal that the game made it possible.
